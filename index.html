How the numbers are worked out (rates as at November 2026)

EPF: employee 11%; employer 13% on wages up to RM5,000 and 12% above. As KWSP requires, contributions follow the Third Schedule wage bands (RM20 steps up to RM5,000, RM100 steps up to RM20,000), so they are worked out on the top of your band and rounded up to the next ringgit. SOCSO (0.5% employee, 1.75% employer) and EIS (0.2% each) apply to wages up to RM6,000; PERKESO uses its own banded tables, so real amounts can differ slightly. LINDUNG 24 Jam (0.75% until 31 May 2028, employee only, wages up to RM6,000) started on 1 June 2026; PERKESO states it is voluntary for Malaysian employees and mandatory for foreign workers. Tax: YA 2026 resident rates (0% to 30%) and the RM400 rebate where chargeable income is RM35,000 or less; PCB is shown as annual tax ÷ 12. Budget 2027 proposes a RM12,000 self relief and lower rates from YA 2027. Living costs: EPF's Belanjawanku 2024/2025 minimum monthly budget for a single person in each city, by public transport or car; the total includes RM150 of savings and assumes a rented room. PTPTN: ujrah is a flat 1% a year on the balance, with PTPTN's standard periods (5 years up to RM10,000, 10 years up to RM22,000, 15 years up to RM50,000, 20 years above); extra payments are modelled as shortening the period, which is our assumption, and PTPTN's repayment page was last updated in March 2022. Retirement: the projection assumes a steady raise, dividend and inflation rate, is shown in today's prices, and compares with EPF's Retirement Income Adequacy targets at age 60 (RM390,000, RM650,000, RM1.3 million). Rates and rules change, so confirm with the official source.

Official sources: EPF Belanjawanku 2024/2025 · LHDN · KWSP (EPF) · PERKESO · PTPTN
